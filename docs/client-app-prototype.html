MyHealth for Ana, the client

A clickable sketch of the phone app for the person being cared for, built on what the API already supports. Ana trains with a personal trainer, follows a dietitian's meal plan and has acupuncture sessions, each professional from a different organization. An assistant in the centre of the tab bar does most of the typing for her: she says what happened and confirms. Tap inside the phone or pick a screen on the left.

The assistant asks before it starts

Using an AI provider means sending health data outside MyHealth, so the first use is a separate, versioned consent that Ana can withdraw, like the therapy consents.

  • Plain lists of what it does and what it never does: no diagnosis, no plan changes, no consent on her behalf.
  • She chooses what it can read (training, nutrition, therapies); only the data needed for each question is sent.
  • Refusing is fine: the whole app keeps working without it.
LGPD sensitive data · provider data processing agreement · RNF-10 no diagnosis

Say it once, confirm with one tap

Ana writes or speaks the way she would to a friend. The assistant turns it into the same records the forms create and shows a card before saving anything.

  • Meal: one sentence becomes a diary entry (lunch, followed in part, shared with Dr. Marina). Tap "Salvar" to see it saved.
  • Pain: it does not assess pain. It offers to send a discomfort report to the trainer and shows when to seek medical care.
  • Questions are answered from her own records, with the source shown.
  • Under the hood each card is an API call made with Ana's own session, so the same access checks, sharing rules and audit apply.
RF-30 diary · RF-19 discomfort report · tool calls to the existing API

Hands-free during the workout

With a bar on her back, Ana says "feito, dez com quarenta e dois e meio". The assistant reads it back as set 3: 10 repetitions, 42.5 kg.

  • Confirm by voice or tap: she says "certo", or taps "Registrar série".
  • Context aware: it knows which exercise and set are current, so short phrases are enough.
  • Rest starts automatically after the set is logged.
RF-17 execution · speech-to-text on the phone, parsing on the server

See the movement, not a description of it

Every exercise has a short muted loop on the workout screen and a full version here, downloaded with the plan so it works with no signal at the gym.

  • Slow motion and captions are one tap each; chapters jump to setup, descent and common mistakes.
  • Front and side angles are recorded for the movements where they matter (squat, deadlift).
  • Who made it: the MyHealth library, or the trainer's own upload for a custom exercise, visible only in that organization.
  • The trainer's note for Ana sits under the video, next to the library's precautions.
Needs file storage and a video CDN (RF-27) · several media items per exercise

Rest time answers the day's questions

Each rest can carry one small question, answered with one tap. The chips under the timer are this workout's questions; tap them to see each one.

  • "Mandou bem!" confirms the set the moment it is logged, like Duolingo's green answer sheet, with a haptic tap on the phone.
  • One per rest, four per workout at most, and never in the last 15 seconds: the card closes by itself and the timer always wins.
  • Asked after recovery: the question waits until the heart rate from the Watch drops below 110 bpm.
  • Every card says who receives the answer. The lunch answer goes to Dr. Marina at another organization; Carlos never sees the diary.
  • Knee "pior" offers a discomfort report to Carlos and says when to see a doctor. The app does not interpret the pain.
  • Sleep and energy are a new data type: they need their own sharing switch and a place in the data export.
Discomfort report (RF-19) · food diary (RF-30) · therapy progress (RF-36) · new check-in record

One glance: what needs me, what's today, what's next

The home answers "what do I do today?" across every professional, in three blocks. Everything else lives one tap away in its own tab.

  • Needs attention appears only when something has a deadline (here, the consent before Friday) and disappears once it's done.
  • Today is one card with one button. The streak sits in its corner; the week detail lives on the Treino path.
  • Next is a short timeline across modules, coloured per module. "Registrar" opens the assistant, which does the typing.
  • Moved out: the assistant card (the button in the tab bar is enough), the week strip (now on the path) and My progress (now in Profile).
RF-06 client home · MT-10 one view across tenants

Notifications that never reveal health data

The texts are the API's real templates. They point to the screen and never show clinical content, because lock screens are public.

  • "Orientação importante" is how a medical-care referral arrives: Ana opens the app to read it.
  • Quick actions on the notification itself: Ana answers "Segui o plano" from the lock screen, without opening the app.
  • Grouped by day, unread marked with a dot; tapping opens the related record.
RF-07 · notification outbox (push or e-mail)

Ana decides who sees what

Sharing is per professional and per data category, with switches she can change at any time. Each change is recorded.

  • Verified badge with council number, so she knows the professional is real.
  • Apple Health can be paused or revoked; saving workouts back to Health is a separate choice.
  • Assistant settings live here too: what it may read, and withdrawing its consent.
  • LGPD actions are plain buttons: download my data, ask for a correction, ask for deletion.
RF-02 sharing grants · RF-38 Apple Health · RNF-04 data export

The plan as a path to walk

Borrowed from Duolingo: the plan becomes a winding path of sessions, in the order Carlos prescribed (A, B, A, C). Ana never has to work out which session is next; the bouncing node is the one.

  • One node per session. Done nodes are filled, today's bounces with "Começar", the rest are locked until their turn.
  • A chest closes each week and the trophy at the end of the phase is the physical assessment (15/12), so progress has a goal she can see.
  • A weekly streak, not a daily one. Rest days are part of training, so the flame counts weeks that met the plan's 4 sessions. A daily streak would push people to train sore.
  • The shield protects a week when Ana reports discomfort or Carlos pauses the plan: getting hurt never costs the streak.
  • XP rewards showing up (sessions, feedback, rest answers), never load or extra sets.
  • A new plan version redraws the path from today. Done nodes stay, because they are execution history.
RF-11 plans · RF-12 versioned publish · RF-19 feedback · RF-24 assessments · new: streak and XP ledger

Every exercise, in any order

Treino A has six exercises and Ana sees all of them before she starts. The list keeps Carlos's suggested order, but she can start with any one, for example when the squat rack is busy. Pick "Session exercises" again after opening the workout to see the same screen mid-workout.

  • Before the workout: each row shows the video thumbnail, sets × reps, load and rest. Tapping a row starts the workout on that exercise.
  • During the workout ("Ver todos" in the workout): done, in progress and still to do, with sets counted per exercise. Here Ana did the leg extension first and is now on the squat.
  • Guidance, not locks: Carlos can mark an exercise "faça primeiro" (heavy compound lifts before isolation work). It shows as a tip and never blocks.
  • New in version 4 is tagged, so the lunge that replaced the leg press does not catch her by surprise.
  • Finishing early is allowed. Skipped exercises go into the summary for Carlos, with a one-tap reason (machine busy, pain, no time).
RF-11 plan items · RF-17 execution logs each set against its exercise, whatever the order

Logging a set takes two taps

Suggested repetitions and load are prefilled from the plan; Ana edits only what differed and taps the check.

  • Lesson-style header: one bar fills set by set across the whole workout (5 of 18), with × to leave and her streak beside it.
  • Exercise strip: six dots (done, current, to do). Tapping any dot or "Ver todos" opens the full list to switch exercise.
  • Live heart rate from the Apple Watch, labelled with its source.
  • Rest timer starts after each set, using the rest time the trainer set.
  • Precautions from the exercise library are shown in place, not hidden in a details page.
  • A looping video of the exercise sits on top; the expand button opens the full video.
  • Tapping the check on the current set starts the rest, which may carry one question.
  • "Falar" opens the voice assistant to log the set hands-free.
  • Pause keeps paused time out of the workout duration. Works offline; the phone creates the ids.
RF-17 execution · RF-40 heart rate during workouts

Finishing is also feedback

Effort on the 0 to 10 scale and a clear "I felt discomfort" switch give the trainer what they need without a free-text burden.

  • Discomfort notifies the trainer and opens a details field.
  • Heart-rate summary shows both the simple and the time-weighted average, with the device, as the API stores both.
  • Save to Apple Health follows her choice in Profile.
RF-18 attendance · RF-19 feedback · RF-41/42 heart-rate means and provenance

Celebrate showing up, not lifting more

Duolingo's lesson-complete moment, adapted to health: the medal, the three tiles and the streak are about consistency, never performance.

  • The XP breakdown is visible, so Ana can see the points come from doing the plan and giving feedback.
  • Discomfort switches records off. Ana reported knee pain, so load records stay hidden until Carlos replies, and the screen says he was told.
  • No leaderboard by default. If leagues ever come, they are opt-in, inside one organization, and ranked by weeks completed.
  • Streak reminders stay generic ("Seu treino de hoje está esperando"): the lock screen never shows health data.
RF-18 attendance · RF-19 discomfort · RF-07 notification templates · new: XP ledger

A meal plan you can follow from the kitchen

Today's meals as a timeline, with quantities in grams or household measures and the substitutions the dietitian allowed.

  • Next meal is highlighted; logged ones are filled in.
  • One tap for the common case ("Segui o plano"), and "Contar ao assistente" for everything else. The form stays as a fallback.
  • Water counted in ml toward the day's target.
  • The dietitian's comment on a diary entry shows under it.
RF-29 versioned meal plan · RF-30 food diary

Logging is about adherence, not calories

The four answers match the API: as planned, partly, something different, or an unplanned meal. A description is optional.

  • "Só para mim" keeps an entry private: it is stored without any organization and no professional sees it.
  • Never shown to trainers: the diary goes only to the dietitian she chose.
  • Photos come later, with file storage.
RF-30 · private entries have no tenant

Therapy with the guard-rails visible

The next session, the care plan's progress and what is blocking: here, a consent to sign before Friday.

  • Qualification badge: "Acupuntura verificada" appears only when the platform verified the practitioner for that modality.
  • After each session Ana reads the guidance and says how she felt on a 1 to 5 scale.
RF-33 qualifications · RF-36 sessions, care plan and client progress

Consent is read, versioned and reversible

The term says which version it is and why she is asked again. Risks are listed separately, and refusing is as easy as accepting.

  • New version, new consent: her yes to version 1 no longer counts once version 2 is published.
  • Withdraw at any time from the therapy screen; the session cannot be completed without a valid consent.
RF-35 procedure consent with version · acceptance test 8

Progress with its method attached

Every measurement says how it was taken, so a change in method is never mistaken for a change in the body.

  • Weight chart from the trainer's physical assessments; the axis starts at 67 kg and is labelled.
  • Attendance for the month and the latest health-history version she submitted.
